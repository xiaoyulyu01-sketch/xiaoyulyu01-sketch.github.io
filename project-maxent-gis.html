Habitat Suitability Modeling and Spatial Zoning Using MaxEnt and GIS

Constructed predictive ecological niche models (ENM) applying the Maximum Entropy (MaxEnt) algorithm to assess spatial environmental suitability for target crops. Integrated high-resolution bioclimatic grids with edaphic physicochemical raster layers, identifying dominant environmental drivers, response curves, and defining spatial zoning boundaries for regional agricultural planning and scientific cartography.

Modeling Algorithm: MaxEnt (v3.4.4) Environmental Layers: Bioclimatic (WorldClim) + Soil HWSD Spatial Cartography: QGIS & ArcGIS Multi-Criteria Overlays

Core Analytical Pipeline Ecological Niche Modeling

1. Layer Harmonization & Screening

Extracted, resampled, and aligned 19 bioclimatic variables with terrain (DEM, slope, aspect) and soil layers (pH, salinity, organic carbon). Filtered multi-collinear layers using Pearson correlation coefficients (|r| < 0.8) and VIF diagnostics.

2. MaxEnt Parameter Optimization

Tuned feature combinations (Linear, Quadratic, Product, Threshold, Hinge) and regularization multipliers to prevent model overfitting. Evaluated predictive performance via Jackknife tests, AUC values, and Boyce indices.

3. Spatial Zoning & Publication Cartography

Transformed continuous probability surfaces into classified suitability tiers (Unsuitable, Low, Moderate, High) using maximum training sensitivity plus specificity (MTSPS) thresholds. Designed publication-ready maps in QGIS.
